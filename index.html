About K Swipe

K Swipe was made by a kidney doctor (Dr D) to help patients and families spot foods that are high in potassium.

Medical disclaimer

This game is for education only. It is not medical advice. Talk to your kidney doctor, dietitian, or care team before changing what you eat. Potassium needs differ from person to person. They depend on your blood test results, kidney function, whether you are on hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis, and your medicines. Some people with kidney disease need more potassium, not less.

How foods are sorted

  • Low: less than 150 mg per serving
  • Moderate: 150 to 250 mg per serving
  • High: more than 250 mg per serving
  • Plates (whole meals): low under about 500 mg, moderate about 500 to 800 mg, high over about 800 mg

Numbers are rounded estimates. Real values vary by brand, variety, ripeness, and cooking. Portion size matters: two servings of a "low" food can add up to a "high" amount.

Sources

  • U.S. Department of Agriculture, FoodData Central (fdc.nal.usda.gov): approximate potassium values per serving
  • National Kidney Foundation (kidney.org): high- and low-potassium food lists and guidance on potassium in kidney disease
  • Product labels for salt substitutes (potassium chloride)
